Data Processing Agreement for Employers

Version 2026-11-12

1. Parties and roles

This agreement is between the business that creates a Wage Tracker employer account (the employer) and [business name] (we, us). It forms part of the Terms of Use and applies while the employer uses Wage Tracker.

For personal data the employer puts into Wage Tracker about its staff, the employer is the controller and we are the processor. Workers' own accounts, and data they keep only for themselves, are covered by our Privacy Policy instead.

3. What we promise as processor

We will:

  1. process the data only on the employer's documented instructions. These terms, and the settings the employer chooses in the app, are those instructions. If an instruction seems to break data protection law, we'll say so.
  2. make sure anyone at our end who can access the data is bound to keep it confidential
  3. keep the security measures in section 5
  4. use sub-processors only as in section 4
  5. help the employer answer staff requests to use their data rights (section 6)
  6. help the employer with security, breach notices, impact assessments and consulting the ICO, as far as is reasonable given what we know
  7. delete or return the data at the end (section 7)
  8. give the employer the information needed to show we meet this agreement, and allow reasonable audits (section 6)

We won't sell the data, use it for advertising, or combine it with other businesses' data except to run the service.

6. Breaches, staff requests and audits

  • Breaches. If we become aware of a personal data breach affecting the employer's data, we'll tell the employer without undue delay, and within 48 hours. We'll say what happened, what data and how many people, likely effects, and what we're doing. The employer decides whether to tell the ICO (within 72 hours) and staff.
  • Staff requests. If a member of staff asks us directly to see, correct or delete data the employer holds, we'll pass it to the employer within 5 working days and help them answer. The app already lets staff see the records the employer keeps about them.
  • Audits. Once a year, or after a breach, the employer can ask for written answers about our security. On-site audits need 30 days' notice, must be reasonable, and are at the employer's cost.

7. When the service ends

When the employer closes its account or stops paying:

  1. The employer can download staff hours, pay and records for 30 days.
  2. After that we delete the employer's staff data within 90 days, including from backups as they expire.
  3. We keep data longer only where the law requires it, and then only for that purpose.
  4. A worker's own account isn't deleted. They keep the hours they recorded themselves and what they could already see about their own work, as the privacy policy explains.

8. What the employer promises

The employer will:

  • have a lawful basis for the staff data it puts in, and tell staff about it in its own privacy notice
  • tell staff before turning on location checks or clock-in photos, and explain why
  • only give owner, HR and manager access to people who need it
  • not put special category data into the app (see section 2)
  • keep its own sign-ins secure and remove access when someone leaves
  • deal with staff data requests, with our help

9. Liability and law

  • Each party is responsible for its own breaches of data protection law. The liability limits in the Terms of Use apply, except where the law doesn't allow them.
  • If this agreement and the Terms of Use disagree about personal data, this agreement wins.
  • This agreement is under the law of England and Wales, and its courts decide disputes.
  • We can update this agreement to reflect changes in law or the service, with 30 days' notice. Changes that reduce protection for staff data need the employer's agreement.
